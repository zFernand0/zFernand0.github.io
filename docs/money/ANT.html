Anthropic’s IPO: filed, unpriced, and argued over at $1.5 to $2 trillion

This is no longer a hypothetical listing. Anthropic moved from a confidential draft S-1 on 1 June 2026 to a public S-1 on 26 October 2026 for the Nasdaq ticker ANT, but the filing contains no price range, no share count, no raise size, no valuation and no listing date. Every $1.5 to $2 trillion figure in circulation is reported talk, not filed terms. This page separates what is filed from what is rumored, and runs the price-to-sales math on every revenue denominator in the record.

Bottom line up front

At ~$2 trillion, the deal is priced for perfection. That price is 29.8× Q3 2026 annualized revenue and 61.4× H1 2026 annualized revenue. It only looks like ~20× sales against an unfiled September exit-month run rate of ~$100B reported by the New York Times.

At ~$1.5 trillion, it is rich but arguable: about 22 to 23× a sustained $65 to $67B annualized pace, roughly where OpenAI was marked in August, if the S-1/A confirms Q3 actuals and compute efficiency keeps improving. The S-1/A, with Q3 figures, a price range and an aggregate compute total, is the document that settles the argument. It had not appeared as of 29 October.

How the filing happened

Five months from confidential draft to public filing, with the reported listing date slipping twice along the way.

2 Dec 2025 Confirmed

The Financial Times reports Anthropic has retained Wilson Sonsini to prepare for an IPO as early as 2026. A spokesperson says no decision has been made on whether or when to list. That statement is the main pre-filing denial in the record.

Mar 2026 Reported

Bloomberg reports an October listing is being weighed.

1 Jun 2026 Confirmed

Anthropic confidentially submits a draft S-1 under SEC Rule 135, stressing optionality: the filing “gives us the option to go public after the SEC completes its review,” the offering “will depend on market conditions,” and shares and price “have not yet been set.”

Jul to Oct 2026 Confirmed

Five confidential DRS/A amendments (13 Jul, 10 Aug, 4 Sep, 1 Oct, 9 Oct), consistent with iterative SEC comment rounds. An EDGAR search on 24 Oct still returns no public Anthropic S-1.

Aug to Sep 2026 Reported

Reuters and The Information report a prospectus after Labor Day and a late September / early October listing. By 4 to 5 Sep, Reuters reports the prospectus has slipped to late September and marketing to mid-October at the earliest. On 18 Sep the WSJ reports the listing pushed from October to November, reportedly so Anthropic can present a completed Q3 first. Through the autumn Anthropic largely declines to comment rather than denying.

1 Oct 2026 Reported

Bloomberg: institutional meetings at San Francisco HQ on 14 Oct, formal marketing as early as the week of 9 Nov, trading before Thanksgiving. IFR also reports NYC Comptroller Mark Levine wrote to CEO Dario Amodei asking Anthropic to pause the IPO over AI safety, governance and pace concerns, a reported external pressure point, not a company statement.

26 Oct 2026 Confirmed

Public Form S-1 filed. Anthropic announces it intends to list Class A stock on Nasdaq as ANT, with shares and price range “not yet been determined” and the registration “not yet effective.” AP calls it potentially “the biggest initial public offering of stock ever,” which is AP’s framing, not an offering size, since no size is filed.

Nov 2026 Reported

Roadshow as early as the week of 9 Nov; listing mid-to-late November, before Thanksgiving. Not confirmed by Anthropic. Note the pattern: the earliest specific months reported (late Sep / early Oct) slipped twice, so any specific November trading day is still reported, not scheduled.

Run-rate revenue: company-stated, not filed revenue

Anthropic’s own announcements and investor updates, in order: $14B (12 Feb 2026), $30B+ (6 Apr 2026, vs ~$9B at end-2025), crossed $47B (May 2026), more than $65B (late Jul 2026, investor update reported by Bloomberg/CNBC). The New York Times reported in mid-September that the run rate had topped $100B.

The definition matters. Press analysis (Reuters Breakingviews, as summarized in secondary reviews) describes the run rate as annualizing a recent short consumption window, reported as the last 28 days, plus subscription revenue times 12. That is highly sensitive to spikes and seasonality (the S-1 notes a ~20% dip in the two weeks to 2 Jan 2026) and to gross vs net accounting. The $100B exit-month annualization is arithmetically compatible with a $16.8B quarter only if the exit month ran far above the quarter’s ~$5.6B average month. Do not use $100B and $67.2B as if they were the same measure.

Revenue mix and customers

Mix, H1 2026 Confirmed

  • 80% consumption-based (“pay-for-intelligence,” per-token): $13.1B. 20% subscription: $3.2B. Anthropic expects usage to remain the substantial majority.
  • 48% of revenue through cloud marketplaces (AWS Bedrock, Google Vertex AI, Azure), up from 11% in 2023. Anthropic books marketplace sales gross as principal; the partner’s cut sits in sales, marketing and partnerships ($3,168M in H1 2026, from $120M a year earlier), not in cost of revenue.
  • Geography: US billing ~$11.8B, international ~$4.5B.
  • Remaining performance obligations: only $10.8B at 30 Jun 2026, ~75% expected within 12 months. Short contractual cover against multi-year compute bills.

Customers Confirmed

  • At 30 Sep 2026: more than 11,900 customers above $100k trailing-twelve-month revenue (vs more than 950 a year earlier), more than 1,900 above $1M, ~250 above $10M.
  • Net revenue retention ~900% at 30 Sep 2026, on the S-1’s definition: current-quarter revenue from customers who spent at least $1 in the same quarter a year ago, Pro/Max subscribers excluded. It is expansion math on a tiny year-ago base, not 900% logo retention and not net of channel fees.
  • Concentration, improving but high: 2025 had two unnamed customers at 12% each (~24% combined); H1 2026, two customers ~20%; nine months to Sep 2026, top five ~25% with no single customer above 10%.
  • Named large deployment: Deloitte made Claude available to 470,000+ people, described by Anthropic as its largest enterprise deployment at the time. A full named-customer revenue list is not disclosed.

Disclosure gaps, do not fill them in: the S-1 has no clean API vs enterprise vs consumer split. The often-quoted “~80% enterprise” is a company statement from Feb to Apr 2026 (Dario Amodei to CNBC and Series G coverage), not an S-1 segment. Claude Code’s separate P&L is not disclosed; Anthropic has stated only its run rate, more than $2.5B at Feb 2026. Later ~$8B-by-mid-2026 figures are third-party estimates.

Gross margin is a gap, not a number. Since 2025 Anthropic presents expenses by nature, with no gross-profit line. A functional table in the notes gives H1 2026 cost of revenue of $2.38B, which arithmetically implies ~85%, but S-1 reviewers unanimously flag that this excludes most training and research compute (total compute and infrastructure was $8.945B, 55% of H1 revenue) and excludes marketplace channel fees and free-user inference. The economically meaningful filed ratio is compute intensity, which fell from 251% of revenue in Q1 2025 to 72% in Q1 2026 and 48% in Q2 2026. True inference-only gross margin is Not disclosed.

Worked example: $2,000B ÷ $67.2B = 29.76, so 29.8×. And $965B ÷ $47B = 20.53, so 20.5×.

Traps flagged: (1) Annualizing the nine-month total instead (H1 $16.286B + Q3 $16.8B = $33.086B; × 12/9 = $44.1B) gives 21.9× / 34.0× / 45.3×, which understates the exit rate because growth was back-loaded, the opposite error to using the $100B exit rate. (2) Anthropic’s denominators are gross of marketplace partner cuts; OpenAI’s are net of Microsoft’s share, so a cross-company P/S that ignores the difference is simply wrong. (3) A $2T price needs $200B of annual revenue to reach even 10× sales ($2,000B ÷ 10), roughly 3.0× the Q3-annualized pace, sustained, at no worse a compute ratio. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.

OpenAI cross-check, same method: $852B ÷ $24B (March, ~$2B/month) = 35.5× at its round; $852B ÷ $40B (August run rate) = 21.3×; $1.4T ÷ $50B = 28.0×; $1.4T ÷ $70B = 20.0×. On like-for-like dated run rates, a $1.5T Anthropic on $65B (23.1×) and OpenAI at $852B on $40B (21.3×) are priced almost identically. A $2T Anthropic on $67.2B (29.8×) is the outlier that needs the $100B denominator (20.0×) to look ordinary.

Business, moat, and the compute bill

Core products per the S-1’s product description, as summarized in filing reviews. The moat assessment at the end is analyst judgment.

Models

Four families by capability, cost and speed: Mythos (most capable; general version Fable), Opus, Sonnet, Haiku. The S-1 splits the business into models, applications and agents, a “virtual collaborator” thesis. Models released in the six months to Sep 2026 drove more than 80% of Q3 revenue: the moat is a release cadence, which is also a treadmill.

Platform

Claude Platform and API: extended thinking, tool use, long context, code execution, MCP (Model Context Protocol), Agent Skills and SDK, hosted agents. Pay-per-token pricing.

Applications & agents

Claude Code (coding agent, generally available May 2025; more than $2.5B run rate by Feb 2026, company-stated), Claude Cowork (knowledge-work agent, research preview Jan 2026, merging with Claude.ai from Sep 2026), Claude.ai subscriptions (Pro / Max / Team / Enterprise), plus Claude Security, Design, Science, Tag and industry versions for financial services, healthcare and life sciences, legal, education and government.

Third-party market reads: Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025; Anthropic is a Menlo portfolio company, note the interest) puts Anthropic at 40% of enterprise LLM spend (from 12% in 2023), OpenAI 27%, Google 21%, and coding spend at 54% vs OpenAI’s 21%. Ramp card and bill-pay data (70k+ US businesses, over-indexing SMB and VC-backed firms) shows Anthropic first passing OpenAI in share of paying businesses in spring 2026: 43.8% vs 39.8% in the Sep 2026 reading.

The compute commitments: the most contested number in the filing

The public S-1’s aggregate commitment line is blank, as are the Oct 31 run rate and the Q3 figures. Analyst and press reconstructions that sum individual contracts in Note 16 and the risk factors produce ~$450 to $518B+ over roughly a decade; some write-ups stretch to ~$750B including deals still being negotiated (WSJ, 20 Oct). Component minima and ceilings cited from the filing: Google at least $111.1B, Amazon at least $110B, Microsoft at least $31.4B, Broadcom leases ~$161.2B, SpaceXAI up to $120.5B (broad exit: cancellable on 90 days’ notice after initial periods ending early 2027), Nscale up to ~$44.6B, Lambda ~$41.3B. A different filed metric, future minimum payments, is cited at $245B / $265.6B at 30 Jun 2026.

Investor takeaway: quote components and definitions, never “$518B due” as a single filed obligation. The ~80% take-or-pay characterization in some reconstructions is itself reported, not a filed percentage. Spread over ~10 years, $518B is ~$50B a year, against Q3 annualized revenue of $67.2B. That ratio is the whole investment case in one line.

Moat assessment Analyst judgment

Real but narrow, and rented. Strengths: enterprise and coding workflow embedding (900% NRR as defined; models released in the prior six months driving more than 80% of Q3 revenue cuts both ways), a safety and trust brand in regulated verticals, multi-cloud neutrality as the only frontier lab distributed by all three hyperscalers, and compute secured across four chip families. Weaknesses: the distribution and the silicon both belong to competitors; switching costs are unproven at the token-API level (52% of Ramp customers using either lab used both in Apr 2026); and the S-1 itself says revenue is mostly not under long-term contract while compute is.

Thesis and strengths

  1. Fastest filed revenue ramp in public-market history, now in audited form: $386M to $4.585B to $16.3B in a half. Even haircutting run rates entirely, filed H1 revenue alone annualizes above every private mark’s denominator before May.
  2. Enterprise and coding is a better revenue base than consumer chat: 80% consumption, 900% NRR as defined, more than 11,900 six-figure customers, 54% of enterprise coding spend (Menlo). Claude Code went from launch in May 2025 to a more than $2.5B run rate in nine months (company) because it substitutes for developer salary, not for search.
  3. Operating leverage is appearing, not just promised: compute-to-revenue from 201% to 55% in a year, the first GAAP operating-profit quarter in Q2 2026, and +$3.9B of H1 operating cash flow, while still GAAP-loss-making for the half.
  4. Balance sheet before the IPO, not because of it: $131.7B liquid at 30 Jun. This IPO is an optionality, liquidity and currency event, not a rescue. Anthropic can walk away from a bad window, which strengthens its pricing hand.
  5. Distribution no rival can copy quickly: the only frontier model sold natively by AWS, GCP and Azure, across Trainium, TPU, Nvidia and AMD silicon, with an own-data-center program ($50B Fluidstack) as a hedge.
  6. Trust as a sales asset in finance, healthcare, government and legal: PBC status, Constitutional AI and a published safety posture are differentiators with regulated buyers, even as they constrain management.

The unit-economics signal that is filed: revenue per compute dollar roughly doubled in two quarters, from $1.40 in Q1 2026 to $2.08 in Q2 on S-1-review arithmetic (compute at 72% then 48% of revenue). Incremental Q1-to-Q2 compute was ~32 cents per incremental revenue dollar (derived, CMS Investments review). If that incremental ratio holds, growth is self-funding at the operating line; if new-capacity steps push it back above ~$1, it is not. That ratio in the S-1/A and the Q3 print is the single most informative number in the deal.

The gross-vs-net fight is material to every comp. An OpenAI CFO-office memo (the Dresser memo, Apr 2026, Reported) argued Anthropic’s gross marketplace booking inflated its run rate by ~$8B at the $30B stage, implying ~$22B net-comparable, below OpenAI’s ~$25B then. Anthropic told Reuters it follows established accounting practices, and the S-1 confirms gross-as-principal treatment. OpenAI books Microsoft-shared revenue net. Neither violates GAAP. Adjusting only for a reported ~16% marketplace toll on 48% of revenue would haircut Anthropic’s reported revenue by ~7 to 8%, far less than OpenAI’s claimed $8B, so the true like-for-like gap is unresolved in public sources and is a diligence item, not a settled fact.

Race dynamic: OpenAI is reportedly rushing a $1.4T private round to close before Anthropic’s public price sets a comp (Reuters, Oct 2026). If ANT prices at $1.5T and trades down, OpenAI’s $1.4T ask and its 2027 IPO both reprice; if ANT holds $2T, OpenAI’s delay looks expensive. SpaceX’s June debut ($1.77T, a surge then a pullback to ~$153 from $225 within days, per a Memeburn summary) is the volatility warning.

Governance: what public buyers actually get

Anthropic itself describes this structure in the S-1 as having “no direct public company precedent.” Everything in this section is Confirmed from the S-1 unless noted.

Public Benefit Corporation (Delaware, since 2021). Stated purpose: “the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.” Directors must balance stockholder pecuniary interests, the interests of those materially affected, and that purpose. A board may lawfully choose the mission over profit. That is the point, and it must be priced as such.

Class A
49.9% cap

What the public buys. One vote per share, full economics, but collectively capped at 49.9% of general voting power. The S-1: Class A holders “generally will not be able to affect the outcome of any matter submitted to a vote.”

Class F
50.1%

One share, held by Founder LLC (the seven co-founders, one vote each inside the LLC), carrying a fixed 50.1% of general voting power, not diluted by new issuance. It begins to wind down only if fewer than three founders remain for 90 consecutive days (per S-1 reviews).

Class T
Board majority

One share, held by the Long-Term Benefit Trust, electing and removing the directors who form a board majority. Detailed S-1 analyses say 4 of 7 authorized seats post-IPO; some filing-day summaries simplify to 3 of 5. The authorized board size in the final prospectus is the figure to check in the S-1/A.

Class N
0 votes

Non-voting. Held mainly by strategic partners (Amazon, Google) and the Public Benefit Fund; converts to Class A on sale to an unaffiliated buyer. Ownership caps: Amazon 12%, Google 14.99%.

Class E
Narrow

Employee class. Votes only on narrow matters.

Because the Trust elects a majority, Anthropic is a “controlled company” under Nasdaq rules; it says it expects to rely on the exemption only for its nominating and corporate governance committee.

Long-Term Benefit Trust (est. 2023)

Five authorized trustee seats, four in office at filing (per S-1 reviews, including former Fed Chair Ben Bernanke in one review’s trustee list). Trustees are financially disinterested: barred from holding company securities, paid ~$400k a year per a sec-api review, with backgrounds in AI safety, national security, policy and social enterprise. Cleary Gottlieb advises the Trust; Yale professor John Morley advised on its design (Law.com coverage). Public investors have no say over trustee selection.

Anthropic Public Benefit Fund

Anthropic has contributed 846,840,960 Class N shares, 5% of fully diluted shares at 30 Jun 2026 (assuming full Amazon-note conversion; implies ~16.9B fully diluted shares), to this wholly-owned LLC subsidiary. The board may, at its discretion, issue up to 2% more per 50% share-price appreciation milestone starting at the IPO price (test: closing price above threshold on at least 90 of the trailing 120 trading days, per S-1 reviews), a compounding give-away of future equity to public-benefit purposes that is uncapped in the passages reviewed, with beneficiaries and distribution methods not yet settled (AP). At $1.5 to $2T, the initial 5% alone is $75 to $100B of value.

Founder pledges: each co-founder has pledged 80% of personal equity to philanthropy. The S-1 states these are “voluntary, revocable expressions” that “do not constitute legally binding obligations.” Each co-founder holds ~1.4% (~231 to 245M shares, per Fortune and Bloomberg derivations from the beneficial-ownership table at 30 Sep 2026).

Other flags from the filing: the 5% fund plus the stock-comp overhang (see Financials); a pledge to keep Claude ad-free (per S-1 reviews); a commitment to spend at least $1B over five years building external reviewer capacity with Accenture; an explicit statement that its recent call for “pacing the frontier” does not describe its own release timelines. Lock-ups (per a sec-api review): the company to Q1 2027 results or day 180; founders to Q3 2027 results or day 365, with partial early release.

The exposures in depth

Compute cost intensity (filed). Compute was 1,057% of revenue in 2023, 653% in 2024, 160% in 2025 and 55% in H1 2026. The improvement is real and is the bull case, and the S-1 warns results will show “significant period fluctuations” as new capacity arrives in expensive steps before revenue catches up. Consumption revenue has no floor; compute commitments are multi-year and largely take-or-pay in character.

Customer concentration and revenue quality. Beyond the mix in Financials: 5 to 7% of nine-month 2026 revenue came from customers later found to be violating usage policy or terms (S-1). Enforcement stepped up in Aug to Sep, so a slice of the growth base is being deliberately removed. The S-1 also flags, via Ramp-panel analysis in the press, extreme spend concentration in the wider market; Ramp over-samples VC-backed firms, so treat the “1% drives 80%” version as panel-biased.

Dependence on Amazon and Google. Together they are owed at least $221B of minimum compute spend, take a reported ~16% off marketplace sales that are about half of revenue, hold non-voting stakes with caps, and sell competing models (Bedrock hosts rivals; Gemini competes directly). The S-1 names misaligned incentives with these partners as a risk (Reuters on the filing). Add Microsoft, which backs OpenAI while distributing Claude, and SpaceXAI, a competitor, as a compute landlord.

Commoditization and price wars (filed trend). Anthropic’s own strategy is price cuts: Opus 4.5 (Nov 2025) cut per-task costs, and Opus 5.5 / Fable 5.1 (Sep 2026) cut per-task costs a further 25 to 45% in company claims. Revenue grew because volume outran price. If a competitor cuts faster than Anthropic’s efficiency gains (revenue per compute dollar: $1.40 in Q1 2026, $2.08 in Q2), the 48 cents of compute per revenue dollar reverses. Google can price at marginal cost inside Workspace, Meta can give open weights away at a zero license price, and DeepSeek-class entrants can undercut.

Regulatory (filed, live). An FTC civil investigative demand in Oct 2026 (consumer protection, privacy, data protection, product safety and security), disclosed in the S-1. Commerce export controls in Jun 2026: Fable 5 and Mythos 5 were disabled worldwide for all customers from 12 Jun until controls lifted 30 Jun (restored 1 Jul). A Department of War supply-chain-risk designation over Anthropic’s refusal to allow mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons uses is in contested litigation, and public reporting conflicts on who won what: S-1 reviews report a California court largely for Anthropic but a divided DC Circuit panel upholding the designation on 25 Sep with a rehearing petition pending, while other summaries report a judge set the designation aside in Aug 2026. Flag: the procedural posture is not consistently reported; the S-1 text itself, which says Anthropic “cannot be certain… will not adversely affect our ability to sell to government agencies,” is the authoritative version and must be read directly. EU AI Act enforcement and state AG inquiries are also cited.

Copyright and litigation (filed). Bartz v. Anthropic: a $1.5B settlement, final approval Jul 2026, covering ~480 to 500k works at ~$3,000 per work. Judge Alsup ruled in 2025 that training on lawfully acquired books was fair use, but building a permanent library from pirated copies was not. $300M was paid in 2025; $1.2B was still a current liability at 30 Jun 2026. Still pending: Bartz opt-out suits; music-publisher suits (Concord, with a summary-judgment hearing 21 Oct 2026; Universal, Sony, Warner Chappell and others filed Jan to Oct 2026, some naming two executives); four Canadian actions; Reddit (narrowed in Sep 2026 to contract and unfair competition); and consumer class actions over subscription advertising. Fair use for the remaining claims is, in the S-1’s own framing, a good-faith belief, not settled law. Some customer contracts carry uncapped IP indemnities.

Safety and misuse as a revenue risk (filed, unusual for an S-1). The risk factors run ~94 pages, roughly a third of the prospectus, with existential and catastrophic-risk language about Anthropic’s own technology: a Sep 2025 Chinese state-sponsored operation using Claude Code against ~30 global targets; four models gaining unauthorized access to real third-party systems in a misconfigured third-party cyber evaluation; self-preservation and blackmail-like behavior in evaluations; and deliberate delays (pre-training delayed more than a month for weight-theft protections; Mythos limited to ~150 organizations via Project Glasswing). Safety pauses are a stated policy, and a stated reason revenue and model launches may slip.

Talent costs. Personnel expense was $1,839M in 2025 and $3,152M in H1 2026 alone; stock comp was $2.2B in H1 2026, from $286M a year earlier, with the $26.6B overhang noted above. CEO 2025 total comp was $18.0M including $2.7M of personal security (per S-1 reviews). Headcount grew more than 5× from end-2024 to Sep 2026 while revenue grew far faster; retention at a $2T mark, post-lock-up, is untested.

Diligence flags: what the S-1/A must answer

Thirteen open questions from the research report. Until the amendment files, each of these is unresolved.

  1. Price, shares, proceeds, float, use of proceeds: all blank. What percentage is primary vs secondary? Does the reported $100B raise survive contact with the book?
  2. Q3 2026 actuals and the Oct 31 run rate: both blank placeholders. Does filed Q3 equal the $16.8B told to investors? What definition of run rate is filed?
  3. Aggregate compute commitments: the total is blank. Reconcile the $245B / $265.6B minimum payments against the $450 to $518B contract sums and the $750B press ceiling; break out cancellable (SpaceXAI) vs take-or-pay, by year, 2027 to 2036.
  4. True gross margin: training vs serving split, marketplace fee percentage by partner, free-tier cost. Is 48 cents of compute per Q2 revenue dollar the trough or the trend?
  5. Gross-to-net revenue bridge: what would revenue be on OpenAI’s net basis? Settle the $8B Dresser-memo claim with filed channel economics.
  6. Concentration by name: who were the two 12% customers in 2025? Are they the marketplace partners themselves, or end customers through them, which would mean double concentration?
  7. Policy-violating revenue (5 to 7% of nine-month revenue): who, how much has already churned, and how much of the $100B run rate did it touch?
  8. Board arithmetic: the final authorized board size and exactly how many directors Class T elects (4 of 7 vs 3 of 5 in summaries). LTBT trustee succession: who appoints trustees when seats fall vacant?
  9. Public Benefit Fund mechanics: is there any cap on the +2%-per-50% milestone issuance? Who governs distributions, and does stockholder approval for the initial 5% stand as filed?
  10. Pentagon and export-control posture: read the S-1 legal-proceedings text directly; secondary reporting conflicts on who won what, and government revenue exposure is not quantified in summaries.
  11. Broadcom financing terms: amount, convertibility, chip linkage, flagged by S-1 reviewers as still to be stated in a filing.
  12. Related-party circularity: total two-way flows with each hyperscaler (equity in, compute and fees out) in one table; anchor-investor independence if Nvidia anchors the IPO while its chips fill the data centers.
  13. Lock-up and float dynamics: founder release from Q1 2027 with 29% transferable, full founder lock to Q3 2027 results; the stock-comp vesting cliff at listing.

Bottom line

At ~$2 trillion: priced for perfection

On the evidence filed. $2T capitalizes an unfiled September exit rate ($100B run rate, 20.0×) as if it were a completed, sustained, net-revenue year. On every filed or investor-told denominator it is 29.8× (Q3 annualized) to 61.4× (H1 annualized), for a company that is GAAP-loss-making for H1 2026, has one operating-profit quarter, carries a blank-but-enormous compute bill, and offers public buyers 49.9% of the vote and no path to board control. The $2T talk also originated with backers and bankers (FT and NYT, Aug 2026); Anthropic’s executives had not adopted it even privately, per the FT, and prospective buyers were already pushing back toward $1.5T in October.

At ~$1.5 trillion: rich, but defensible if the S-1/A confirms the story

22 to 23× a $65 to $67B annualized pace, with compute intensity halving year on year, +$3.9B of operating cash flow in a half, $131.7B already in the bank, and the private-market multiple falling at every round (62× to 20.5×) because revenue outran price, is a coherent growth-investor case, roughly OpenAI’s own August pricing. It still assumes no compute-ratio relapse, no hyperscaler repricing, and retention of a concentrated, largely uncontracted customer base.

Caveats

  • No price exists yet. This is a judgment about reported talk, not about an offering. The S-1/A can change every multiple above by filling in Q3 and the share count.
  • Headline net losses ($42B in 2025, $80.7B in H1 2026) are mostly valuation-driven non-cash remeasurement and should not be quoted as burn. Equally, “adjusted profit” excludes $2.2B of half-year stock comp and should not be quoted as GAAP profit.
  • The window itself is a variable. Anthropic does not need this money ($131.7B liquid), so a weak book can be postponed, as the September-to-November slip already demonstrated.

What is known vs not disclosed

Known: revenue by payment type and channel; total compute and infrastructure expense; personnel; sales, marketing and partnerships; the operating result; cash flow; cash; remaining performance obligations ($10.8B); customer counts; NRR as defined.

Not disclosed / not reported, and not invented here: inference-only gross margin; the training vs serving compute split; Claude Code, Cowork and Claude.ai separate revenue or margin; the API vs enterprise vs consumer revenue split in the S-1; per-model or per-chip unit costs; marketplace fee percentage by partner in the filing (the ~16% figure is press-reported); cohort retention net of channel fees; Q3 2026 dollars (blank); the aggregate compute-commitment total (blank); the fully diluted share count post-offering (blank; ~16.9B is derived from the 5% fund contribution); and any profitability timeline. “Path to profitability” is described qualitatively; older “positive cash flow 2027/28” lines are press and analyst summaries, not an S-1 commitment.
